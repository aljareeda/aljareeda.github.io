## 🎓 A rebuilt two-year tech diploma: 32 fields, strict pass rules, and a zero for any missed exam

Morocco has issued a full rewrite of the **Higher Technician Diploma** (BTS in French). It is a two-year, post-baccalaureate vocational degree that leads to a job, a business, or further university study. It matters to families abroad because a foreign baccalaureate counts: the order says "baccalaureate or equivalent." Applicants must not be older than 23 at the end of September of the application year. Anyone who did not study Arabic in their baccalaureate can be exempted from the Arabic exam.

**What you can study.** The order lists 32 specialties in three clusters:
- **Economics, management and services (13):** accounting, banking and insurance, digital marketing and e-commerce, tourism management, sports management, culinary arts and others.
- **Electrical and IT engineering (7):** renewable energy and energy efficiency, networks, app development, full-stack web and **artificial intelligence development**.
- **Mechanical and civil engineering (12):** industrial maintenance, car maintenance, water treatment, energy engineering, civil engineering (building option), agricultural production and others.

**How it works.**
- **Admission:** each school picks candidates by reviewing baccalaureate results through a local committee. Written or oral tests can be added for some fields.
- **Structure:** four semesters over two years, in three blocks (languages and communication, science and technology, professional skills).
- **Internships:** these are mandatory, at least 4 weeks in year one and 6 weeks in year two.
- **Year-one transfer:** a student's overall average splits 50% continuous assessment and 50% a local exam.
- **Final diploma:** 25% continuous assessment and 75% a national unified exam.
- **Passing grade:** an overall average of at least 10/20, with no "disqualifying" mark. Below 9/20 in the languages block or the science block is disqualifying. For the professional block, every component needs at least 9/20.
- **Honours:** Passable (10 to under 12), Assez bien (12 to under 14), Bien (14 to under 16) and Très bien (16 and above).

**The harsh rules.** A zero in any component counts as a disqualifying mark. A zero is also given to anyone who misses a test, **even with a valid excuse**, though they can still sit the remaining tests. Anyone caught cheating can be barred from the diploma exam. Repeating year one is not allowed, but a student can apply to a different specialty, except the one they failed. Year two can be repeated once, by a reasoned decision of the class council. Failed candidates can re-sit as "free" candidates, twice in a row, and can keep passed components for two years. Students who spent two or more years in training and failed can request an administrative certificate proving it.

The order replaces the 2012 rules on the same diploma. It applies from the academic year printed as "2026/2025", which most plausibly means 2025–26. The gazette does not clarify, and since it is published in October 2026 the effective date is worth checking with the school.

*Source: Order no. 1432.26 of the Minister of National Education, Preschool and Sports, dated 9 July 2026.*

## ⚖️ Justice Ministry creates a unit for seized and frozen assets, plus a cybersecurity unit

The Justice Ministry is adding a **Division for Managing Seized Assets and Property**, which gives the state a dedicated office for the money and goods held in criminal cases. It will oversee seized assets stored at court clerks' offices across the kingdom, handle their return, and destroy seized items that are worthless, unusable, unsellable or environmentally dangerous. It will also manage assets frozen at foreign judicial request or under United Nations Security Council sanctions on terrorism and weapons proliferation. It must build a central database of seized and frozen assets and standardize evidence storage offices.

A second new **Information Security Division** will set the ministry's cyber policy, manage risks, run continuity plans and report incidents to the National Cybersecurity Authority. The gazette's excerpt omits unchanged lines, so it does not say which central directorate the seized-assets division sits under.

*Source: Order no. 2172.26 of the Minister of Justice, dated 13 October 2026, amending order 1501.22 of 19 October 2022.*

## 🏛️ New names at the top: government signing powers are being redone

Several orders in this issue show a changed leadership. They cite a royal decree of 1 October 2026 naming **Fatima Zahra Mansouri** as Head of Government, a royal decree of 21 October 2026 appointing government members, and a new decree of 26 October 2026 on the Secretary-General of the Government's powers. Orders from early September were still signed by Aziz Akhannouch, and the Secretary-General now signs as Hassan Tarek. One 27 October order refers to a "Minister of Economy, Finance and Public Efficiency", a title the September orders do not use. The gazette gives no explanation of the transition.

On 26 October Mansouri delegated administrative and financial signing authority to her chief administrative and finance officer. It covers staff paperwork, spending orders, contracts, and four special state funds: investment promotion, basic telecom service, support for the national human development initiative, and social rehabilitation. The Official Printing Office director was also named as an authorizing officer for her office's own budget.

*Sources: Orders nos. 3.56.26 to 3.60.26 of the Head of Government (26 October 2026); orders 2183.26 to 2191.26 and 2190.26 of the Secretary-General (27 October 2026).*

## 🩺 12 private medical, dental and pharmacy specialty programs approved; two private universities renamed

The Higher Education Ministry approved **12 specialist programs at a private university in Rabat**, the Zahraoui International University for Health Sciences (UIASS). They are:
- anatomical pathology;
- clinical pharmacy and industrial pharmacy;
- orthodontics and prosthodontics;
- cardiology, dermatology and rheumatology;
- ophthalmology and ENT (five years each after the first six study years);
- restorative and surgical dentistry (four years each).

Approvals run to the end of the 2026–27 academic year for the first group, 2027–28 for most of the second, and 2028–29 for ophthalmology and ENT. Programs are listed by "academic year of the list" in two orders, covering lists for 2023–24 and 2024–25.

Two private universities also change names from the **2026–27 academic year**:
- **Private University of Health and Sciences of Agadir** becomes **Private University of Agadir**.
- **Ibn Rochd International Private University** (Casablanca, Sidi Maarouf) becomes **HEM International Private University**.

Both must display the words "private university" on their buildings and all documents, and will issue diplomas under the new names. Families abroad should check that new transcripts carry the correct name.

*Sources: Orders nos. 1908.26 and 1909.26 (7 September 2026); 1798.26 (11 August 2026); 1947.26 (11 September 2026), of the Minister of Higher Education.*

## 💸 Treasury officials get set limits for waiving state debt costs: 5,000 and 50,000 dirhams

The Economy and Finance Ministry updated who can forgive costs on money owed to the state. A named regional official in Marrakech-Safi can waive **collection costs up to 5,000 dirhams** and **late-payment surcharges up to 50,000 dirhams**. The surcharge power excludes civil-servant salaries and wages. Other orders name officials who can authorize forced collection in Oujda, Jerada, Casablanca-Anfa, Salé and Meknes.

*Sources: Orders nos. 2019.26 and 2020.26 of the Minister of Economy and Finance, dated 1 September 2026.*

## 🌪️ A road director gets power to sign up to 80 million dirhams in disaster-fund contracts

The Interior Minister delegated authority to the Director General of Roads to approve and cancel contracts, and to sign warning notices, under the **Fund for Combating the Effects of Natural Disasters**, in amounts up to **80,000,000 dirhams**. A named deputy covers absences. The order replaces a December 2024 delegation.

*Source: Order no. 2081.26 of the Minister of the Interior, dated 29 September 2026.*

## 🛣️ Who spends road money, and who can fine

The Equipment and Water Minister named regional and provincial transport officials across all 12 regions as assistant spending authorities. They can spend from the general budget and two special funds: the **Special Roads Fund** and the **Fund for defining and protecting the maritime and port public domain**. They also issue payment and collection orders for:
- temporary occupation fees for public property;
- annual billboard fees on state roads;
- sand-sale fees;
- fines for extracting building materials without a permit;
- administrative fines under the quarry law (Law 27.13);
- repair of damage to public property.

*Source: Order no. 2043.26 of the Minister of Equipment and Water, dated 4 September 2026.*

## 🚧 Land taken for two roads

Two public-use orders allow expropriation for road works. In Benslimane province, a regional road (no. 313) is to be widened to three lanes between kilometer points 12+000 and 13+000 in Fedalat commune. The table lists a plot of roughly 1,303 square meters and records competing claims over surface-use rights. In Khenifra province, provincial road 7302 is to be built between points 9+200 and 9+610 in El Hammam commune. It takes two plots of 1,625 and 684 square meters.

*Sources: Orders nos. 2032.26 and 2033.26 of the Minister of Equipment and Water, dated 21 September 2026.*
