## 🗺️ Six tribal-land tracts, about 16,160 hectares, get official boundary-mapping dates

If your family holds a share in tribal land in the Boulemane or Tata areas, this is the one to read. Six decrees in this issue schedule the official drawing of boundaries (called "administrative delimitation") on collective land, meaning land owned jointly by tribal communities (known as *Soulaliyate* land) rather than by individuals. Adding up the approximate areas stated in the decrees gives about **16,160 hectares**. Field work starts between **28 and 31 December 2026**, and each decree says it may continue on following days if needed.

| Tract | Province | Community that owns it | Approx. hectares | Work starts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sahb Lharmel | Boulemane | Rekou Fgous (ركو فقوس) | 7,921 | 30 Dec 2026 |
| Ain Lakseb | Boulemane | Rekou Fgous | 4,328 | 28 Dec 2026 |
| Tandit 10 | Boulemane | Rekou Fgous | 2,608 | 29 Dec 2026 |
| Sahb Ben Hussein | Boulemane | Ouled Jerrar (أولاد جرار) | 1,226 | 31 Dec 2026 |
| Wandit | Boulemane | Rekou Fgous | 64 | 31 Dec 2026 |
| Tahat Roqba w Ighir (تحت الرقبة وإغير) | Tata | Tamachat (تماشت) | 13 | 29 Dec 2026 |

*Place and community names are transliterated from Arabic, so local spellings may differ.*

Each tract contains farmland and grazing land, including irrigation water where it exists. On the start date, an official delimitation committee meets at 9 a.m. at the local administrative headquarters (the Tandit office for the five Boulemane tracts, the Adis office for the Tata tract) to begin the work. The decrees only set the calendar. They do not explain what happens to individual members' rights afterward, so relatives at home should ask the local authority or their community representatives. This story overlaps land ownership and agriculture, and it is ranked here for its direct relevance to families with ancestral land.

*Decrees 2.26.695 to 2.26.700, 22 September 2026, issued under Law 63.17 on delimitation of collective land.*

## 🏭 Morocco's phosphate giant OCP gets two logistics subsidiaries for its Safi corridor

The state-owned phosphate group OCP (Office Chérifien des Phosphates) has been authorized to create two companies it will own **100%**, named **Mzinda Logistic I** and **Mzinda Logistic II**. The first will handle transport and cargo handling between the Mzinda industrial area and the coastal city of Safi. The second will handle storage at Safi's industrial and port facilities. They are part of a programme to turn the Mzinda–Safi axis into an integrated industrial corridor (the programme's Arabic name is «مزيندة - مسقالة», and its English spelling is not given in the source). The stated aims include securing flows between production sites, processing plants and export ports, using low-carbon technology, and lining up partnerships with specialised operators and investment funds. OCP's board approved the plan on 11 March 2025, and the authorization is valid for one year from publication.

*Decree 2.25.1094, 22 September 2026.*

## 🛣️ A 25 km widening of National Road 16 in Fahs-Anjra keeps its legal green light

The government renewed the "public interest" declaration for turning National Road 16 into a wider dual road between **kilometre 10 and kilometre 35**, a 25 km stretch in Fahs-Anjra province. The original declaration dates from 23 May 2024. Renewing it keeps the project's legal standing, which is what allows land along the route to be acquired for it. The decree gives no cost, contractor or construction timeline.

*Decree 2.26.706, 22 September 2026.*

## 🛡️ Allianz Maroc is absorbed by Sanlam, and the merged insurer takes a new name

Morocco's insurance regulator has approved the merger of two well-known insurers. Allianz Maroc is absorbed by Sanlam Maroc, which continues operating as **Sanlam Allianz Maroc**, and Allianz Maroc's operating licence is withdrawn. The regulator's president approved the merger on 2 July 2026 and its board made the decision on 15 July 2026. The decision also cancels an earlier 2022 ruling that had renamed Saham Assurance as Sanlam Maroc. The gazette does not say what happens to existing policies, so customers should ask their insurer directly.

*Decision P/EA/4.26 of the Insurance and Social Welfare Supervisory Authority (ACAPS), 15 July 2026.*

## 💥 An underground explosives depot for up to 30,000 kg is approved in Rahamna province

The energy and sustainable development minister authorized the company CADEX (Société Africaine des Explosifs) to build a permanent underground "double" explosives depot in Jbilat commune, Rahamna province. It may hold at most **30,000 kg** of first-class explosives or the equivalent. It cannot start operating until a further ministerial decision is issued. The company must post written safety rules that ban smoking and anything that can spark or ignite, require protective clothing, and set out emergency steps. The permit lapses if work does not start within a year, or if work is stopped for more than a year.

*Ministerial decision 1697.26, 17 July 2026.*

## 🏫 Land is expropriated for a new "Maghreb Al Kabir" middle school in Rahamna

A decree declares a new public middle school, "Maghreb Al Kabir", to be in the public interest and takes over one plot of **8,350 square metres** in Ait Hammou commune, Rahamna province. The government's property directorate is delegated to carry out the expropriation. A public inquiry ran from 28 May to 30 July 2025. The decree does not mention compensation amounts, the school's capacity or an opening date.

*Decree 2.26.603, 22 September 2026.*

## 🌧️ Fifteen plots, 2,616 m², to be given up for rainwater drains in Salé

A regional decision allows 15 plots in the Technopolis area of Salé's Ihssine district to be acquired for rainwater drainage channels. Together the plots cover **2,616 square metres**, based on the areas listed. Most are unregistered farmland held by heirs of the presumed owners, and one plot's owner is listed as unknown. A public inquiry ran from 29 April to 4 June 2026, and the right to expropriate is given to the Salé council president and the director of the Bouregreg Valley development agency.

*Decision 1830.26 of the Rabat-Salé-Kénitra regional governor, 13 August 2026.*

## 🎓 Four military academies rewrite how their degrees work

Six joint decisions signed on 22 May 2026 amend the study and grading rules at the Royal Military Academy, the Royal Naval School, the Royal Air School and the Royal Military Administration School. At the Royal Military Academy, the bachelor's runs for six semesters and 45 units, and a unit is passed at 10 out of 20 with no element below 6, from the 2025–2026 academic year. The Royal Air School's bachelor's offers three tracks (navigation and intelligence, air traffic control, aviation systems) and its engineering degree has two tracks, each with 35 units and a final project. The Royal Military Administration School's master's in administration and finance has 28 units and 1,400 hours in total. These are military institutions, and the decisions cover course structure and grading, not admissions.

*Joint decisions 1010.26 to 1015.26, 22 May 2026.*
