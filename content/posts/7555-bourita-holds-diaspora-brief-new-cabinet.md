# Bourita Holds the Diaspora Brief in Morocco's New Cabinet

*Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco's Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7555, dated 26 October 2026 (14 Jumada al-Ula 1448). This aljareeda translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.*

## 🌍 One minister now answers for both foreign affairs and Moroccans abroad

If you are a Moroccan living abroad, this issue tells you who is in charge of your file. Nasser Bourita is named Minister of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation and Moroccans Residing Abroad. The decree gives him the powers of two separate government authorities: foreign affairs and African cooperation, and Moroccans residing abroad and migration affairs. He also takes authority over all the offices and departments created under the earlier decrees for those two areas (decrees 2.24.957 of 21 November 2024 and 2.14.192 of 4 April 2014).

The decree does not announce new rights, fees or procedures for the diaspora. It settles who signs off on consular matters, migration policy and diaspora programs. It is one of 21 decrees in this issue that do the same for the whole new cabinet. All of them say they take effect from **16 October 2026**. That is five days before the dahir (royal decree) of 21 October 2026 appointing the ministers, and the source does not explain the gap.

The government is headed by Prime Minister Fatima Zahra Mansouri. Her appointment dahir is dated 1 October 2026, and the cabinet appointment dahir is dated 21 October 2026.

*Overlap: this is also an accountability story, because it shows who is formally responsible for each area of public life.*

<small>Decree 2.26.815 of 26 October 2026 (see also 2.26.814 to 2.26.834)</small>

## 💰 The finance minister gets the subsidy fund and the social security fund

Fouzi Lekjaa is Minister of Economy, Finance and Public Efficiency. He takes state oversight of the **Compensation Fund**, the state fund used to hold down the price of basic goods. He also takes oversight of the **National Social Security Fund (CNSS)**, which handles social security for private-sector workers.

The decree also delegates to him the power to order payments from a special treasury account that supports competition, consumer protection and market regulation. That account was created under the 1985 Finance Law. He also takes over the public policy convergence and evaluation directorate, which previously sat under a different ministry.

<small>Decree 2.26.819 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 💧 Water, forests and desertification now sit with one minister

Nizar Baraka is Minister of Equipment, Water and Forests. Beyond his existing powers, he now sets state policy on protecting and developing national forests, fighting desertification, and creating and managing protected areas. He also acts as secretary of the **Higher Council for Water and Climate**, the body set up by Morocco's water law (Law 36.15).

Water is a high-stakes subject for families and landowners back home. This decree gives forests, water and infrastructure a single political owner.

<small>Decree 2.26.820 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 🚆 The national railway comes under the transport minister

Abdessamad Kayouh, Minister of Transport and Logistics, takes state oversight of the **National Railways Office (ONCF)**, Morocco's national rail operator. The decree says this lasts until the text that created ONCF is amended. He also oversees the other public bodies that fall under his ministry.

<small>Decree 2.26.827 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 🏥 Health minister also drafts national social protection policy

Younes Bejijou (as transliterated from the Arabic) is Minister of Health and Social Protection. He is tasked with preparing the state's social protection policy and monitoring its implementation. He must also make sure the different government social protection programs line up with each other. The employment minister, Abderrahim Chahid, keeps labor and social dialogue but is expressly excluded from social protection.

<small>Decrees 2.26.822 and 2.26.829 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 📰 Press and media oversight sits with the culture and youth minister

Mohamed Mehdi Bensaid is Minister of Youth, Culture and Sports. He also takes the powers over the **communication sector**, which covers media and press. He also oversees the public bodies under both the culture and communication authorities, and must prepare an integrated youth policy. The communication sector's own founding decree (2.24.1143 of 11 April 2025) is cited as the basis for these powers.

*Overlap: this touches freedom of the press and expression, because it names the minister with state oversight of the sector.*

<small>Decree 2.26.825 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 🎓 Kindergartens move to the education minister

Mohamed Nabil Benabdallah, Minister of National Education, Preschool, Vocational Training and Upskilling, takes over powers related to kindergartens (*riyad al-atfal*). These were previously held by the youth and sports authority. He also oversees the regional education and training academies. Separately, Azzedine El Midaoui becomes Minister of Higher Education, Scientific Research and Innovation.

<small>Decrees 2.26.821 and 2.26.826 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 🤖 Artificial intelligence gets a named minister

Ahmed Rahou is Minister of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Transition and Administrative Reform. He prepares the government's AI policy and is the authority responsible for telecommunications and the digital economy under the 1997 postal and telecommunications law (Law 24.96). He also oversees the Digital Development Agency.

<small>Decree 2.26.831 of 26 October 2026</small>

## 🏭 Business, investment and food sovereignty

Riad Mezzour is Minister of Industry, Trade, Investment and Economic Integration. He now holds both the industry and trade authority and the investment authority, with oversight of the public bodies under them. Khalid Hatimi is Minister of Agriculture, Sea Fisheries and Food Sovereignty, and is tasked with preparing and implementing government policy on food sovereignty. Charafat Afilal covers Energy, Mines and Sustainable Development, and chairs the National Environment Council.

<small>Decrees 2.26.823, 2.26.824 and 2.26.828 of 26 October 2026</small>
