## 🗳️ Spreading election deepfakes or fake news now carries 2 to 5 years in prison

Morocco has rewritten the rules for its next parliamentary election, and the biggest change targets digital manipulation. Anyone who uses social media, open broadcast channels or **artificial-intelligence tools** to spread false news meant to divert votes, or to push people into not voting, faces **2 to 5 years in prison and a fine of 50,000 to 100,000 dirhams**. A new article carries the same penalty for sharing a doctored video, audio or photo of a voter or candidate without their consent, or for sharing fabricated claims meant to damage their privacy or reputation. Creating false content to undermine the integrity of the vote is punished the same way.

Other penalties in the same law:

- **Election-day campaigning online:** posting campaign posters or leaflets on election day, including through social media or AI tools, is punishable by 3 to 6 months in prison and a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 dirhams. For civil servants who do it on the job, the penalty rises to 6 months to 1 year and 50,000 to 100,000 dirhams.
- **Foreign-platform ads:** paying for political ads or electoral leaflets on foreign platforms or websites is punishable by 3 to 6 months in prison and 10,000 to 20,000 dirhams.
- **Vote-buying:** giving or taking gifts or money to sway votes carries 2 to 5 years and 50,000 to 100,000 dirhams. A conviction also removes the right to vote for five years and bars the person from running for two successive parliamentary terms.
- **Public money:** candidates must file campaign accounts with the Court of Audit within 90 days of the results. Public campaign funds that are neither justified nor returned to the Treasury are treated as embezzlement.

**Rules for candidates:**

- Candidacies are filed through an online platform, with paper originals still deposited in person.
- Independent candidates need at least 200 certified voter signatures per seat.
- Regional lists must consist exclusively of women.
- Independent lists of candidates aged 35 or under can receive public funding of up to 75% of their campaign spending.
- Anyone caught in the act committing a crime, or certain listed offences, is barred from running. A sitting member detained for six months or more loses their seat.

**For Moroccans abroad:** the law spells out proxy voting for voters living outside the Kingdom. You fill in the proxy online, entering your national ID number, your consular registration number and details of the person who will vote for you. You then confirm the details are accurate, download the form and send it to your proxy by email or post. Candidates living abroad must supply proof of residence from their country and a copy of a valid residence permit. The published text is abridged in places, so the exact documents are not fully visible in this issue.

The law applies to the first House of Representatives election held after its publication. The Constitutional Court ruled it constitutional on 24 December 2025.

<small>Source: Dahir n° 1-25-70 of 16 January 2026, promulgating Organic Law n° 53-25. The Arabic text appeared in BO n° 7478 of 29 January 2026.</small>

## 🌍 Moroccans abroad can register to vote online, and will be notified by email

A companion law rewrites how voter lists work. Citizens inside or outside Morocco can request registration, or transfer their registration, at the local authority, at an embassy or consulate, or through the government's voter-list website, **listeselectorales.ma**. A separate interior ministry order (13 March 2026) sets the website's schedule:

- It accepts registration and transfer requests **1 April to 31 December** each year.
- It also opens **18 to 24 January** for the annual revision, and for revisions before a general or by-election.
- The site lets registered voters check their own details.
- Voters can add an email address or mobile number.
- People who have just received their electronic national ID card for the first time may be contacted by email or phone and told to register.

Practical points that matter to the diaspora:

- Decisions on applications from Moroccans abroad are now sent to them by email.
- Refusals must be explained and communicated within three days.
- If a voter living abroad ends up registered twice, the **most recent** registration is kept.
- Voters who move to another municipality without requesting a transfer can be removed from the list automatically, with notice sent to the address on their ID card.

<small>Source: Dahir n° 1-25-72 of 9 January 2026 (Law n° 55-25); Interior Ministry order n° 503-26 of 13 March 2026.</small>

## 🏛️ Political parties face tougher audits, with dissolution as the penalty

Parties that take state money must now prove how they spent it. The Court of Audit can send a formal notice, after which a party has **45 days** to file its accounts or fix the problem. Money used for the wrong purpose or left unjustified must go back to the Treasury. A party that fails to file its annual accounts **three years in a row** can be taken to court for dissolution, and the court must rule within 30 days. The Court of Audit can also refer breaches in the use of public funding to the public prosecutor. Parties must hold a national congress at least every four years. The law also sets a diaspora-related condition for annual state support, appearing to require that a woman candidate living abroad head at least one of the party's regional lists. The published text is abridged at that point, so the exact wording is unclear.

<small>Source: Dahir n° 1-25-71 of 16 January 2026 (Organic Law n° 54-25).</small>

## 👧 Morocco's child-labour report: down from 517,000 to about 101,000, but still concentrated in the countryside

The national advisory body on economic, social and environmental affairs (CESE) published its opinion on eradicating child labour. The bulletin's figures:

- The number of working children aged 7 to 17 fell from about **517,000 in 1999 to about 101,000 in 2024**, or 1.3% of children in that age group.
- The problem remains concentrated in rural areas: **2.5%** of rural children work, against **0.5%** in cities.
- Worldwide, the International Labour Organization estimates about 138 million children aged 5 to 17 work, 54 million of them in dangerous jobs.

The report links the problem to poverty, school dropout and the large informal economy. It sets out five recommended axes, covering governance, enforcement of the law, social support for vulnerable families, education and training pathways, and wider involvement of actors. The opinion was adopted on 25 May 2026. It is advice to the authorities rather than a binding law.

<small>Source: CESE opinion "Pour une politique publique effective d'éradication du travail des enfants au Maroc", published in BO n° 7544.</small>

## 📊 Two new laws guarantee the independence of Morocco's statistics, and fine people who ignore official surveys

Two laws create a new framework for official numbers. The first defines a national statistical system and requires the authorities to decide independently, with confidentiality for individual answers. The second reconstitutes the **High Commission for Planning (HCP)** as a public body based in Rabat, with its own administrative and financial autonomy. It must work with independence, impartiality, objectivity and transparency, and take a regional view. It is tasked with producing and publishing statistics and national, regional and sector accounts, and with setting up an electronic platform for sharing statistical data with ministries and other bodies. There is also a consequence for ordinary people: ignoring an official survey questionnaire, or knowingly giving a false answer, is a fine of **200 to 500 dirhams** for individuals and households, and **600 to 1,200 dirhams** for companies. The fines double for a repeat offence within 12 months.

<small>Source: Dahir n° 1-26-71 of 28 July 2026 (Law n° 046-26) and Dahir n° 1-26-70 of 28 July 2026 (Law n° 047-26).</small>

## 🌊 Ksob irrigation water priced at 0.64 dirham per cubic metre

Farmers in the Ksob irrigation area (Essaouira province) will pay a set "equilibrium rate" of **0.64 dirham per cubic metre of water, excluding VAT**. This matters for diaspora families who hold farmland in the area.

<small>Source: Joint order n° 1439-26 of 13 July 2026.</small>

## 🐟 19 fish and seaweed farms lose their licences for sitting idle; one new farm is approved off Sidi Ifni

The government declared **19 aquaculture farms vacant**, meaning their authorisations lapse, after inspections found them unused for more than a year. The farms named in the bulletin include seaweed, oyster and fish operations, several of them in the Dakhla area. In the same issue, a company called Fisheries Ecosystems Sarl received a **10-year, renewable** agreement to run a **25-hectare** floating-cage farm at sea off Sidi Ifni. It will raise sea bass, gilthead bream and meagre. It will pay a fixed fee of 12,500 dirhams a year plus 1/1000 of the value of what it sells.

<small>Source: Joint orders n° 1456-26 to 1472-26 (14 July 2026) and n° 1537-26 to 1539-26 (28 July 2026).</small>
