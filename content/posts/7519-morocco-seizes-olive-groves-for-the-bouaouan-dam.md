## 💧 Morocco orders farmland and olive groves seized for the Bouaouan dam

Most of this issue, roughly 70 printed pages, is a land-seizure order for the Bouaouan dam in Sidi Ghanem commune, Chichaoua province. The government has declared the dam a matter of "public utility", the legal step that lets the state take private land for a project. For families with land in the area, this is the notice that matters: the plots are now formally listed and marked for expropriation.

The decree lists the plots in a numbered table of ownership accounts, which runs up to account 169. Each entry gives the owner, the plot number, its area, its land-registration status and what stands on it. Most plots are listed as non-registered land. Most are farmland, either irrigated or rain-fed, planted with olive, almond, carob, pomegranate and fig trees. A smaller number carry houses, outbuildings, wells and cisterns. A second table lists other rights holders, including heirs, co-claimants and one entry for the religious endowments authority (Habous). The land is marked in red on three plot maps at 1:2000 scale attached to the decree.

The notice follows a direct public inquiry held at the commune's offices from 22 January to 22 March 2025. The decree's title marks it as a supplementary ("tatima") decree, but the Gazette does not say which earlier text it completes. It also gives no compensation amounts, no dam capacity, no cost and no timeline. Those details will have to come from other sources.

*Source: Decree No. 2.26.389 of 4 June 2026, signed by Head of Government Aziz Akhannouch and Equipment and Water Minister Nizar Baraka.*

## 🎓 Three foreign degrees get a path to Moroccan recognition

If you studied abroad, three decisions published here matter to you. Morocco's higher-education minister accepted three specific foreign qualifications for equivalence, meaning they can be treated as the equal of a Moroccan degree.

- **Qatar:** a bachelor's in law and police sciences from the Police Academy (Police College) in Qatar is accepted as equivalent to a Moroccan law licence (bachelor's level).
- **France:** an engineer's title from the Conservatoire national des arts et métiers (CNAM), specialty building and public works, is accepted as equivalent to the Moroccan state engineer diploma in civil engineering.
- **Canada and USA:** a master of engineering in civil and environmental engineering from the University of Western Ontario, combined with a bachelor of science in civil engineering from Wentworth Institute of Technology, is accepted as equivalent to the state engineer diploma.

Each decision names one exact diploma, so a different degree or specialty is not covered. The decisions followed advice from the national equivalence committees, which met on 23 April 2026 and 30 September 2025.

*Source: Decisions Nos. 991.26, 992.26 and 993.26 of 20 May 2026, signed by Minister Azzedine El Midaoui.*

## 🏥 Eight health-training institutes now have set capacities: 540 seats each

Morocco has rewritten the rules for its vocational health-training institutes. Each of the eight institutes has a capacity of 540 seats, made up of 300 in the "technician" track and 240 in the "qualification" track. By my arithmetic that is 4,320 seats nationwide. The institutes are in Casablanca, Rabat, Fès, Marrakech, Oujda, Agadir, Tangier and Laayoune, and the Laayoune institute covers all three southern regions.

The technician track has five specialties:
- patient transport and emergency care
- surgical-theatre assistant
- medical-equipment cleaning and sterilization technician
- medical-social secretary
- emergency reception and triage assistant

The qualification track has four: care assistant, hospital accommodation and hygiene assistant, infant and child care assistant, and elderly-care assistant. Technician programs run 2,500 hours and qualification programs 1,500 hours, with hands-on placements making up the bulk. For surgical-theatre assistants, 1,300 of the 2,500 hours are placements. The new rules apply from the 2024–2025 school year, which means they take effect retroactively.

*Source: Health Ministry Decision No. 347.26 of 9 February 2026, signed by Minister Amine Tahraoui.*

## 🗳️ The left alliance gets "the Letter and the Candle" for the 23 September 2026 election

Ahead of the legislative elections on 23 September 2026, the Interior Ministry assigned a ballot symbol to the "Left Alliance". The alliance is made up of the Democratic Left Federation and the Unified Socialist Party. Its symbol is "the Letter and the Candle" (as the Arabic reads in translation). The alliance's candidate lists will carry this symbol on ballots.

*Source: Interior Minister's Decision No. 1190.26 of 18 June 2026, signed by Abdelouafi Laftit.*

## 🏙️ A new urban plan for Saada, near Marrakech

The government approved a new development plan and zoning rules for Saada commune in Marrakech prefecture. It replaces the earlier plan for the commune's center, which was approved in December 2021. It also declares the plan to be of public utility. Under Morocco's planning law that kind of declaration is typically what lets authorities acquire land set aside for roads and public facilities, though the decree itself does not detail any acquisitions. Anyone holding or buying land in Saada should check how the new zoning applies to their plot.

The plan went through a public inquiry from 22 October to 20 November 2025. The communal council discussed it on 27 November 2025, and a committee reviewed public objections on 23 December 2025.

*Source: Decree No. 2.26.347 of 11 June 2026.*

## 🏫 Land for a new lower-secondary school in Tangier

The Economy and Finance Minister authorized the transfer of a 3,328 m² plot in Tangier for building the Ibn Tawit Al-Tanji lower-secondary school. The plot belongs to a private company. The Gazette notes it carries two forced mortgages in favor of the Tangier-Medina tax collection office, one first-ranking and one second-ranking. A direct administrative inquiry ran from 21 May to 23 July 2025.

*Source: Ministerial Decision No. 971.26 of 13 May 2026, signed by Nadia Fettah.*

## ⚖️ The tax-offenses committee gets a new chair

The Head of Government changed the membership of the committee that examines tax offenses. The committee's new chair is Mounir El Montasser Billah, a counselor at the Court of Cassation and Secretary General of the Superior Council of the Judiciary. The Gazette prints only the first lines of the amended article, and the rest of the membership is marked "no change". The source does not say who he replaces or why.

*Source: Head of Government Decision No. 3.28.26 of 1 June 2026.*
