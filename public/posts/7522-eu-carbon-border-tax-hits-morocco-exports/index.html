EU's Carbon Border Tax Kicks In — Morocco's Fertilizer Exports First in the Crosshairs

August 18, 2026
French Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7522, dated 2 July 2026 (16 moharrem 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

💶 The EU’s Carbon Border Tax Is Now Live — and It’s Already Targeting Morocco’s Fertilizer Trade

Europe’s new carbon border tax (known as MACF in French, CBAM in English) took effect in January 2026, and Morocco’s official economic advisory body has just published its assessment of the fallout. The mechanism forces importers of carbon-intensive goods — steel, aluminum, cement, nitrogen fertilizers, hydrogen and electricity — to pay a fee at the EU border matching the carbon cost European producers already pay under the bloc’s internal emissions-trading system. The goal is to stop companies from dodging Europe’s climate rules by manufacturing dirtier goods elsewhere and shipping them in.

Morocco has a lot riding on this: the EU absorbed 62% of the country’s trade in 2023, making it Morocco’s single most important economic partner. For now, the direct exposure is contained — the European Commission estimates that only 3.7% of Moroccan exports to the EU fall under the new tax, concentrated almost entirely in nitrogen fertilizers, which alone make up 2.9% of everything Morocco sells the bloc. But the report warns that’s a floor, not a ceiling: Brussels plans to widen the mechanism to indirect emissions and additional product categories in the coming years, and other countries are rolling out their own versions of the same tax, which could compound the effect.

The Economic, Social and Environmental Council (CESE), which produced the opinion, frames this as a fork in the road rather than a pure threat. State phosphate giant OCP has already begun investing in green ammonia production and decarbonizing its operations — proof, the Council argues, that Moroccan industry can adapt and even turn the new rules into a competitive edge. But it also warns that companies without the money or technical know-how to calculate and certify their carbon footprint risk being frozen out of the EU market by rising administrative costs and fees. One telling detail buried in the report: the EU itself will pocket 75% of all CBAM revenue collected worldwide for its own budget — an estimated €1.4 billion a year.

The Council’s recommendations include setting up a national CBAM steering unit, creating a support fund to help small and medium exporters cover carbon-accounting costs and green investments, building university and vocational training in carbon-footprint calculation, and accrediting carbon auditors through Morocco’s standards body (IMANOR). It also floats the idea of Morocco eventually adopting its own domestic carbon-pricing system, partly to keep any carbon revenue at home rather than handing it to Brussels.

(Avis du Conseil Économique, Social et Environnemental sur l’impact du MACF, adopté le 28 août 2025 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

💧 France Lends Morocco €100 Million for Its National Water Strategy

Morocco has signed a €100 million (about $108 million) credit agreement with the French Development Agency (AFD) to help fund the country’s national water policy, at a moment when drought and water stress remain front-of-mind for families with land, farms, or relatives back home. The loan, agreement number CMA 1349 01 N, was signed on 8 May 2026 and formally approved by government decree on 23 June 2026. The bulletin does not break down exactly which water projects the funds will go toward — only that they support Morocco’s broader national water strategy, which in recent years has centered on new dams, desalination plants, and water-transfer infrastructure between river basins.

(Décret n° 2-26-489 du 23 juin 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

✈️ Tanger Med Gets a Radio Frequency for This Year’s Marhaba Operation

If you’re one of the hundreds of thousands of Moroccans abroad who drive home each summer through Tanger Med, this one’s for you. Morocco’s broadcasting regulator (HACA) has authorized Tanger Med Port Authority to temporarily broadcast on FM frequency 100 MHz for the duration of Marhaba 2026 — the annual state-run operation that manages the summer rush of expats returning by car and ferry — running from 1 May through 31 October 2026. The station is meant to carry only content directly tied to the crossing operation, such as live rebroadcasts of SNRT and 2M news bulletins to keep travelers informed. It must go silent during the parliamentary election campaign period, 10–22 September 2026, and faces fines of 5,000 to 20,000 dirhams per day for violations of the terms.

(Décision du CSCA n°31-26 du 17 mars 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

🛢️ Morocco Leases 17,000 km² of Atlantic Seabed for Oil and Gas Exploration

Morocco’s energy ministry has granted ten new offshore hydrocarbon exploration permits — all named “Gharb Deep Offshore,” numbered I through X — jointly to the state hydrocarbons office (ONHYM) and Murphy Morocco Oil Co, a subsidiary of the US oil company Murphy Oil. Together the ten blocks cover roughly 16,990 km² of the Atlantic continental shelf off Morocco’s northwest coast, near Kénitra and Larache — an area larger than Kuwait. Each permit runs for an initial three-year exploration period starting 3 April 2026. In the same bulletin, the ministry also approved a fifth amendment to the “Guercif Onshore” petroleum agreement with UK-based Predator Gas Ventures, and extended the “first complementary period” for two “Lixus Offshore” exploration permits held by Chariot Oil & Gas.

(Arrêtés n° 997-26 à 1006-26 du 19 mai 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

🦁 Morocco’s Environment Watchdog Warns Its Biodiversity Is Eroding Faster Than Its Laws Can Keep Up

Morocco is home to a striking amount of wildlife for its size — 25,597 recorded animal species, nearly 7,500 plant and fungi species, and over 7,000 marine species across an exclusive economic zone larger than 1.2 million km². But the country’s top advisory council warns that legal protections haven’t kept pace with the pressure this biodiversity is under from urbanization, unregulated farming expansion, pollution, and climate change: the share of Moroccans living in cities jumped from 51.4% in 1994 to 62.8% in 2024, squeezing natural habitat in the process. Iconic species like the Atlas lion are now critically endangered in the wild. A biosafety and benefit-sharing law meant to regulate genetically modified organisms and protect against biopiracy still hasn’t been adopted, and many of the country’s protected natural reserves still lack formal, legally binding management plans.

On the positive side, the report credits Morocco’s forest strategy with reforesting 150,000 hectares since 2022 — a quarter of the way to its 600,000-hectare target for 2030. The Council is calling for an urgent, dedicated biodiversity framework law and a new national coordinating body reporting directly to the Head of Government, to stop biodiversity policy from being scattered piecemeal across ministries.

(Avis du Conseil Économique, Social et Environnemental sur la biodiversité au Maroc, adopté le 22 décembre 2025 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

🏦 A State Bank Still Can’t Close Its Books After 13 Years in “Liquidation”

Bank Al-Maghrib, Morocco’s central bank, has extended — for the seventh time since 2013 — the liquidation deadline of the Banque Nationale pour le Développement Économique (BNDE), a former state development bank. The latest extension, granted in June 2026, gives the liquidator one more year, pushing the process past the 13-year mark with no indication of when it will actually wrap up. The central bank’s decision offers no explanation for the repeated delays beyond noting that liquidation operations “have not been closed.”

(Décision du Wali de Bank Al-Maghrib n°164 du 8 juin 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

📻 Broadcast Regulator Suspends Radio Show Over Insult to National Football Player

Morocco’s audiovisual regulator (HACA) issued a formal warning to Radio Mars and ordered a one-week suspension of its program “الحقيقة في 90 دقيقة” after a recurring host used an insulting and demeaning term against a Moroccan national football team player while commentating on the Africa Cup of Nations final. The station was also ordered to read a public apology statement on air every day during the suspension. Separately, state broadcaster SNRT received its own formal warning over unrelated content shown on its flagship “Al Aoula” channel, though the bulletin does not detail the underlying complaint.

(Décisions du CSCA n°20-26 et n°30-26 du 17 mars 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

🚌 Only 1 in 9 Rural Trips in Morocco Uses Formal Transport

A new advisory report paints a stark picture of mobility in rural Morocco, where 13.7 million people — 37% of the population — live across more than 1,280 rural communes covering over 90% of the national territory. Despite decades of road-building programs that have spent more than 62 billion dirhams (roughly $6.3 billion) since the 1990s, only 11.1% of rural trips today use formal public transport; most people instead rely on motorbikes and bicycles (14.8%), personal vehicles (5.9%), or informal, often unregulated transport (5.7%). The report notes some real progress — road deaths are down 29% since 2015 — but flags aging, overloaded vehicles, patchy regulation, and weak coordination between authorities as ongoing barriers, problems that disproportionately affect women and the most vulnerable rural residents. The World Bank is separately funding a $250 million, five-year program aimed at improving rural public transport.

The Council is recommending that Morocco formally classify its network of unclassified rural roads, write specific rural-driving rules into the traffic code, and give regions the transport-planning powers they’re already legally entitled to but rarely use.

(Avis du Conseil Économique, Social et Environnemental sur le transport rural, B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

📺 Three New Pay-TV Services Get the Green Light

Morocco’s broadcasting regulator authorized three new pay-TV and streaming offerings to operate in the country: “Orange TV” for telecom operator Medi Telecom, “Inwi Live” for Wana Corporate, and a distribution license for “TOD,” the streaming platform of beIN Sports MENA.

(Décisions du CSCA n°47-26, 48-26 et 49-26 du 16 juin 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)

🏭 Trade Defenses: 5-Year Antidumping Duty on Egyptian Steel

Morocco’s industry and finance ministries imposed a definitive antidumping duty, lasting five years, on cold-rolled steel sheet imports from Egypt, following a review by the country’s import-surveillance commission. In a separate move, the ministries also updated the list of developing countries exempted from an existing safeguard duty on coated wood-fiber panels, revising the roster of over 100 countries that qualify for the exemption.

(Arrêtés conjoints n° 1164-26 et 1165-26 du 8 juin 2026 — B.O. n°7522 du 2 juillet 2026)
