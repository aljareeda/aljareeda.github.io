Morocco Greenlights Land for New High-Speed Rail Line to Marrakech

July 20, 2026

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7527, dated 20 July 2026 (5 Safar 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

🚄 Land Cleared for the Kénitra–Marrakech High-Speed Rail Line

Morocco has taken a concrete legal step toward building its second high-speed rail line, this one linking Kénitra to Marrakech. Two decrees declassify forest land along the route near Mohammedia, moving it out of the state forest domain and into the public domain specifically so the National Railway Office (ONCF) can build the new track.

The first decree affects a 3-hectare, 45-are, 91-centiare plot in the Beni Yakhlef commune; the second covers 9 hectares, 26 ares, 55 centiares split across the Chellalate and Mohammedia communes — together roughly 12.7 hectares. The Ministry of Transport and Logistics will pay a combined 5,797,000 dirhams (about 4.84 million and 955,260 dirhams respectively) into the state’s reforestation fund as compensation for the lost forest land. The line would extend Morocco’s TGV network beyond the existing Tangier–Kénitra corridor, deepening the country’s high-speed rail footprint ahead of the years to come.

Source: Decrees №2.26.431 and №2.26.440, dated 16 Muharram 1448 (2 July 2026).

🎓 A Wave of New University Faculties, Mostly in the South and the Rif

A government decree quietly redraws the map of Moroccan higher education, adding dozens of new faculties and schools to universities across the country — many in regions that have historically had little university infrastructure. Sidi Mohamed Ben Abdellah University (Fez) gains new law, science, economics, and language faculties in Taounate and Taza. Mohammed I University (Oujda) adds a law faculty in Nador and, in Berkane, a new medicine/pharmacy/dentistry faculty, a social sciences faculty, a National School of Artificial Intelligence and Digitalization, and a food-industry and biotechnology school.

The biggest expansion is at Ibn Zohr University (Agadir), which picks up new faculties of applied sciences, law, languages, humanities, economics, medicine, pharmacy, and dentistry spread across Ouarzazate, Taroudant, Smara, Tiznit, Ait Melloul, and Kelmim, plus new AI/data-science, technology, and advanced-technology schools in Laayoune and Dakhla. Moulay Ismail University (Meknes) expands into Errachidia, Abdelmalek Essaadi University (Tetouan) adds faculties in Ksar El Kebir and Larache, Chouaib Doukkali University (El Jadida) adds one in Sidi Bennour, and Sultan Moulay Slimane University (Beni Mellal) expands into Khouribga. For Moroccan families abroad with children studying — or hoping to study — outside the traditional big-city universities, this is a real widening of options closer to home regions.

Source: Decree №2.26.486, dated 16 Muharram 1448 (2 July 2026), amending decree №2.90.554.

🏥 Morocco Sets an “Age Limit” on Refurbished Medical Scanners

A Health Ministry decree sets, for the first time, clear rules on how old a used or refurbished piece of medical equipment can be before it can no longer be registered for sale in Morocco. The cutoff is half of the device’s total expected service life (as set by the manufacturer) or half of its maximum rated operating hours, calculated at the moment the registration request is filed.

The rule covers high-value diagnostic and treatment equipment: X-ray machines, CT scanners, MRI machines, gamma cameras, SPECT and PET scanners, PET/CT and PET/MRI hybrid systems, and external radiotherapy, brachytherapy, and radiosurgery equipment using ionizing radiation. Any such device must also already be registered in Morocco as new equipment, with its safety, quality, and intended use unchanged. It’s a consumer- and patient-safety move aimed at keeping aging imported diagnostic and cancer-treatment machines out of Moroccan hospitals and clinics.

Source: Decree №630.26, dated 29 Ramadan 1447 (19 March 2026).

📜 Over 25,000 Hectares of Tribal Land Officially Recognized

Four decrees close out long-running administrative land-demarcation processes for “Sulaliyate” (collective/tribal) land, formally classifying the plots as belonging to their respective tribal communities. In Taroudant province, the “Third Nouman” land (1,160 hectares) and “Ait Slimane” land (3,616 hectares) are confirmed for their tribal communities. In Figuig province, the “Lkhrouiaa” land (5,449 hectares) and “Bejrad” land (15,065 hectares) go to the Ouled Nasser tribal community. Altogether, more than 25,000 hectares are locked in as recognized collective property — a process that matters directly to Moroccan families abroad whose ancestral land rights run through tribal, rather than individual, title.

Source: Decrees №2.26.391, №2.26.392, №2.26.393, and №2.26.394, dated 16 Muharram 1448 (2 July 2026).

🤝 Two Charities Get Public-Utility Status and Bigger Asset Ceilings

The Abdel Rahman Foundation, based in Rabat, had the ceiling on the property and assets it’s allowed to hold raised to 100 million dirhams. Separately, the newly recognized “Federation for the Development of Chronic Kidney Failure Patient Associations” in Morocco’s Oriental region (based in Oujda) was granted official “public utility” status, letting it hold up to 25 million dirhams in property and run one public fundraising campaign a year without needing prior government authorization — it only has to notify the General Secretariat of the Government 15 days in advance. Public-utility status also typically comes with tax benefits, a boost for a group representing kidney patients in a region with real dialysis and treatment-access needs.

Source: Decrees №2.26.519 and №2.26.542, dated 16 Muharram 1448 (2 July 2026).

🏗️ Six Land Expropriations Clear the Way for New Local Infrastructure

A batch of decrees expropriates small parcels of private and community land to make way for public facilities: a bypass upgrade to Regional Road N°3015 connecting the Tit Mellil–Berrechid highway (Mediouna province); a new primary school in the Sakhour Rahamna commune (Rahamna province); a new qualifying high school in Beni Mellal; a new security post/police station in Chefchaouen; a new second administrative annex in Tahla (Taza province); and a new qualifying high school in Fez. Landowners are listed by name in the decrees along with compensation notes (mortgages, easements, ongoing disputes) attached to several plots.

Source: Decrees №2.26.341, №2.26.432, №2.26.421, №2.26.422, №2.26.423, and №2.26.424, dated 16 Muharram 1448 (2 July 2026).
