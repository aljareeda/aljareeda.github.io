Morocco Opens 12 New Offshore Oil Blocks to Foreign Drillers

May 21, 2026
French Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7510, dated 21 May 2026 (4 Hija 1447). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

🛢️ Morocco Hands Out 12 Offshore Oil Exploration Blocks

The government approved two petroleum agreements between the Office National des Hydrocarbures et des Mines (ONHYM) and foreign oil companies, opening up 12 offshore exploration blocks along Morocco’s Atlantic coast.

The bigger deal is brand new: Murphy Morocco Oil Co., Ltd signed an agreement (approved 3 April 2026) for the “Gharb Deep Offshore” zone, made up of 10 exploration permits (Gharb Deep Offshore I through X). Separately, Chariot Oil & Gas Holdings (Morocco) Limited and its partner Chariot Offshore Gas Limited (formerly Energean Morocco Limited) got a third amendment to their existing “Lixus Offshore” deal, covering permits I and II — extending their first exploration extension period by 12 months while trimming a second extension period by the same amount, and revising the work program.

No financial figures (investment size, expected output) are disclosed in the text.

Sources: Arrêté n° 752-26 and n° 753-26 of 15 chaoual 1447 (3 April 2026)

📡 Fiber-Sharing Deal Aims to Open Up Morocco’s Broadband Market

Morocco’s telecom regulator (ANRT) approved a “reference offer” from Uni Fiber — a joint venture set up by Maroc Telecom (Itissalat Al-Maghrib) and Inwi (Wana Corporate) — that requires it to share its fiber-optic (FTTH) network with any telecom operator that asks, on equal terms.

Under the decision, Uni Fiber must: give competing operators access to its fiber links and connection points; provide access points for mobile antennas to help expand mobile coverage and avoid duplicate infrastructure; set up an automated system to handle orders and service requests from other operators; and regularly report deployment data to the regulator. Uni Fiber cannot favor any single operator.

Source: Décision ANRT/DG/N° 01-26 du 10 kaada 1447 (28 April 2026)

🔥 Butane Gas Distribution Margins Adjusted

The economy ministry updated the fees and margins built into Morocco’s subsidized butane gas supply chain, effective retroactively from 1 March 2026. For cylinders under 5 kg, packaging costs are set at 619 DH/ton, distribution company margins at 510 DH/ton, and depot margins at 255 DH/ton; for cylinders over 5 kg, the figures are 553, 467.50, and 205.83 DH/ton respectively.

The text sets margins for packagers, distributors, and depots in the supply chain — it does not explicitly state whether this changes the final price ordinary households pay at the till.

Source: Arrêté n° 936-26 du 20 kaada 1447 (8 May 2026)

🐟 Also in This Issue

  • Aquaculture expansion: Six new marine farm concessions authorized (10-year renewable agreements) along the Atlantic coast — seaweed farming off Safi (Taiba Seafood, 15 ha), fish farming (sea bream, sea bass, meagre, amberjack) off Essaouira (Zniber Seafarm, 25 ha), shellfish farming off Agadir province (Extramer, 15 ha), plus concessions for Imar Aqua and two for Aquadune (algae and fish farming).
  • Medical diploma recognition: More than a dozen ministerial orders add specific foreign university degrees to Morocco’s officially recognized-equivalent lists for the general medical doctorate and for cardiology, neurology, endocrinology, radiology, and trauma-orthopedics specialties — relevant to Moroccan doctors trained abroad.
  • Insurance license renewals: Regulator ACAPS renewed operating licenses for two Rabat-based mutual insurers, Mutuelle Centrale Marocaine d’Assurances and Mutuelle Attamine Chaabi.
  • Corporate law tweak: A decree adjusts what a statutory auditor’s special report must contain when a public company (SA) waives shareholders’ preferential right to subscribe to a new share issue.
  • Aquaculture governance: Two joint ministerial orders set minimum representativity thresholds (51% and 55% of national production) for professional bodies to be recognized as the official “interprofession” for Morocco’s shellfish and fish/shrimp aquaculture sectors.
