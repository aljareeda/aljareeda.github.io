Morocco Is Embedding Judges in Its Embassies to Speed Up Diaspora Cases

Morocco Is Embedding Judges in Its Embassies to Speed Up Diaspora Cases

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7513, dated 1 June 2026 (15 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

⚖️ Judges are moving into Moroccan embassies, and cross-border cases are their job

If you have an unpaid alimony order, a custody fight that crosses a border, a Moroccan judgment a foreign authority won’t act on, or a relative’s estate stuck between two countries, a new decree is aimed at problems like yours. It creates the “liaison judge” (qadi al-ittisal): a Moroccan magistrate attached to a Moroccan diplomatic mission abroad, working directly under the head of that mission.

In civil, commercial, administrative and family matters, the judge follows the cooperation requests that Moroccan courts send to their counterparts abroad and works to speed them up. The decree’s list reads like a diaspora checklist:

  • mutual recognition and enforcement of judgments
  • serving court papers
  • mixed marriages
  • recovering children and exercising visitation and custody rights abroad
  • collecting alimony abroad
  • legal aid abroad
  • settling the estates of Moroccans who die abroad
  • the civil side of international child abduction

In criminal matters, the judge follows extradition files, transfers of convicted prisoners, formal complaints, requests for judicial assistance, and the service of criminal court papers. The judge also advises the embassy and consulates on legal questions, helps prepare joint consultative committee meetings with the host country, and can propose new treaties or updates to existing ones.

Selection is formal. The Higher Council of the Judicial Power (the judges’ self-governing body) publishes a call for candidates with at least 15 days to apply. A committee with one representative each from the Council, the Justice Ministry and the Public Prosecution interviews them. Each candidate must present a plan for strengthening judicial cooperation with the host country. The committee shortlists at most three candidates, ranked by merit. A joint decision of the Council’s president-delegate, the Justice Minister and the Foreign Minister names the winner. If nobody qualifies or applies, the Council’s president-delegate can propose a judge directly.

The term is four years, extendable one year at a time with renewals capped at three. The judge must report to the ambassador, needs the ambassador’s approval to travel, and files an annual report by the end of January. That report goes to the Council, which passes a copy to the Justice Minister and the head of the Public Prosecution. The Foreign Ministry provides the office and administrative staff, and the Council’s budget covers other costs.

The decree does not say how many judges will be posted or in which countries.

Source: Decree No. 2.25.970, 14 May 2026.

🛃 Customs gets a new organisation chart, with a desk for Moroccans abroad

The agency that runs Morocco’s border checks and customs duties is reorganised into five directorates:

  • Studies, Taxation and International Cooperation
  • Procedures, Partnership and Dispute Settlement
  • Customs Control and Investigations
  • Resources
  • Information Systems

A companion ministerial order restates the divisions and services under each. Several of them matter to travellers and the diaspora:

  • a service for “special regimes and Moroccans residing abroad”
  • a passenger-control service
  • an e-commerce control service
  • a National Customs Brigade, with units against drug trafficking and money laundering

The texts also list a service for investment and industrial acceleration zones, a customs laboratory and a digital innovation centre. They do not explain what each unit will do day to day, so the practical effect on travellers remains to be seen.

Source: Decree No. 2.26.270, 30 April 2026; Order No. 939.26, 7 May 2026.

🌾 Seven decrees fix the boundaries of about 13,470 hectares of tribal land

Seven decrees approve the official boundary-marking of land owned collectively by tribal communities (Soulaliyate land) and classify it permanently as their property. Four are in Taourirt province and three in Fqih Ben Salah province. Families with roots in these communities now have officially fixed boundaries for their collective land. The biggest parcels are Bled Beni Koulal at 6,982 hectares and Ajfirat, belonging to the Sajaa community, at 4,641 hectares. The others are:

  • the Beni Fachet commons: 485 hectares
  • Es-Sbaï Khokhat, for the Zoua community: 791 hectares
  • three Ouled Hatan parcels: 203, 66 and 304 hectares

The decrees rest on Law 63.17 on the administrative delimitation of collective land. Land registrars certified each operation in November 2025.

Source: Decrees No. 2.26.353 to 2.26.359, 21 May 2026.

💧 A new dam in Safi and three drinking-water projects get their land

The state is acquiring land for one dam and three drinking-water schemes.

  • Safi province: the Ouled Salem dam (works zones 1 and 2, plus the reservoir) will be built in the Laamamra and Nkka communes. The decree covers dozens of owners’ parcels, many planted with argan, olive and fig trees. The dam’s capacity is not given.
  • Kelaat Sraghna province: two decrees cover drinking water for the towns of Kelaat Sraghna and Attaouia and neighbouring communities, drawn from the “Madariya” canal. One covers 13 plots and the other a single 3,797 m² plot of collective land.
  • Errachidia: two plots totalling 4,681 m² in Khank commune will strengthen the town’s water supply. One carries a note about a partial objection from the State Property delegate.

In the three drinking-water decrees, the National Office of Electricity and Drinking Water gets the power to carry out the expropriations. The texts give no compensation amounts.

Source: Decrees No. 2.26.361 (21 May 2026), 2.26.316, 2.26.317 and 2.26.333 (14 May 2026).

⚡ A power substation near Khemisset and a wider road in Berkane

The National Office of Electricity and Drinking Water will build a 60/225 kV transformer substation in Sidi El Ghandour commune, Khemisset province, on six plots totalling about 7,868 m², with olive trees, a well and some buildings on the land. In Berkane province, expropriation clears the way to double provincial road 6015 over 1.85 km (from km 11+000 to km 12+850) in Fezouane commune, after a public inquiry from 12 February to 14 April 2025.

Source: Decrees No. 2.26.136 (21 May 2026) and 2.26.186 (14 May 2026).

🌲 Forest land swapped, and a gendarmerie post made legal

In Midelt province, 178 hectares of state forest are swapped one-for-one for 178 hectares of the Ait Ouafella community’s collective land, with no cash payment either way. In Guercif province, 7,000 m² of Mezguit forest moves into the state’s private property to regularise the Royal Gendarmerie post in Sakka commune. The land is valued at 175,000 dirhams (25 dirhams per m²). The National Defence Administration pays that sum into a state fund used to buy land for reforestation.

Source: Decrees No. 2.26.322 and 2.26.337, 14 May 2026.
