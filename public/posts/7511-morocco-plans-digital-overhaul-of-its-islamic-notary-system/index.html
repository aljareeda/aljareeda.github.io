Morocco Plans Digital Overhaul of Its Islamic Notary System

May 25, 2026
Arabic Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7511, dated May 25, 2026 (8 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

⚖️ Morocco wants to drag its 700-year-old Islamic notary system into the digital age

The biggest item in this issue isn’t a new law yet — it’s a unanimous opinion from Morocco’s Economic, Social and Environmental Council (CESE) on a draft law that would rewrite the rules for Adouls, the traditional notaries who certify marriage, divorce, inheritance, wills, and property deals under Islamic law. This matters well beyond legal circles: Adouls are the officials many Moroccans, including those abroad, have to go through for family-status paperwork — marriage certificates, divorce records, inheritance shares — so how this profession is modernized directly affects how easy or painful those procedures are.

There are currently about 3,134 practicing Adouls in Morocco, of whom roughly 16% are women. The current law governing them, No. 16.03, is 20 years old, and the new draft (No. 16.22, running 203 articles across 15 chapters) is meant to replace it. The CESE’s recommendations, adopted unanimously on March 25, 2026 after hearings with the Justice Ministry, the national Adoul body, and civil-society groups, include:

  • Building a secure digital platform with blockchain-based electronic signatures and a national “sovereign cloud” to store contracts, making documents tamper-proof.
  • Allowing the “Lafif” testimony (a traditional multi-witness certification used when no other proof exists) to be taken remotely through secure digital tools, and explicitly recognizing women’s testimony within it — closing a gap that currently leaves this open to inconsistent practice.
  • Raising the bar to enter the profession to a Master’s-level degree in law or Islamic jurisprudence, plus mandatory continuing training.
  • Boosting women’s representation in the profession’s leadership bodies, in line with Morocco’s constitutional gender-parity principle.
  • Giving Adouls a clear, fast right of appeal when a judge refuses to authorize their documents, and a proper legal mechanism to hold client funds in escrow.
  • Using AI tools to help Adouls with legal drafting and keeping up with case law, without replacing their professional judgment or liability.

The council also flagged a structural weakness: the draft law requires 21 separate implementing regulations to actually take effect, with no firm timeline set for issuing them — meaning even if parliament passes it, the real-world impact could be delayed for years unless the government moves fast on the follow-up rules.

Source: CESE opinion on draft law №16.22 on the organization of the Adoul profession, published in Bulletin Officiel №7511.

🌾 Bread wheat import duty gets turned back on, then off again

A decree (№2.26.419, May 25, 2026) reinstates Morocco’s import duty on soft wheat and its by-products (customs codes 1001.99.00.19 and 1001.99.00.90) for a six-week window, from June 1 to July 31, 2026. Then, as an exception to this year’s Finance Law, the same decree suspends that duty again starting August 1, 2026. Morocco relies heavily on imported wheat and regularly toggles this tariff to manage bread prices and supply, so the net effect for consumers is a temporary summer swing before the duty-free import regime resumes. The order is signed by Prime Minister Aziz Akhannouch alongside the ministers for budget, agriculture, and industry and trade.

Source: Decree №2.26.419 of 8 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447 (May 25, 2026).

🕌 Government offices closed for Eid al-Adha

State administrations and local governments will exceptionally shut down on Friday, May 29, 2026, to mark Eid al-Adha (the Feast of Sacrifice). It’s a routine annual measure, but worth knowing if you have business with a Moroccan government office around that date.

Source: Head of Government decision №3.27.26 of 5 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447 (May 22, 2026).
