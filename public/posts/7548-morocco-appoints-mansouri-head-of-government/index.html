Morocco Appoints Fatima Zahra Mansouri as Head of Government

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7548, dated 1 October 2026 (19 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

🏛️ Fatima Zahra Mansouri is named Head of Government, effective 29 September 2026

The Head of Government is the office that signs Morocco’s implementing decrees, including every government decree printed in this same issue. A royal decree (dahir) published in the gazette now names Fatima Zahra Mansouri to that post under Article 47 of the Constitution. It takes effect from 17 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448 (29 September 2026), two days before the text was signed in Rabat on 1 October 2026, so the start date is retroactive.

The same issue shows the handover in the paperwork. Decrees dated 16 and 22 September 2026 are issued by the “Head of Government” and signed by Aziz Akhannouch. Mansouri’s name appears on those decrees as the countersigning Minister of Land-Use Planning, Urban Planning, Housing and City Policy.

The royal decree itself has only two articles: the appointment and the order to publish it. The gazette does not say why the change happened, who else is affected, or what becomes of the rest of the cabinet. Watch for follow-up appointments in later issues.

Source: Dahir No. 1.26.76, 1 October 2026 (19 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448).

🏠 State housing aid: a 30-day online route to release your mortgage

People who bought a primary residence with state housing aid now have a defined process to get a mortgage release certificate (the document that lifts the lien, Arabic rafʿ al-rahn). The beneficiary files a request on the government’s online platform, using a form prepared by the housing ministry. The body in charge must examine it within 30 days of filing. If the legal conditions are met, the certificate is delivered through the same platform.

The decree also lets the State entrust that body with managing the financial aid itself and issuing the release certificates. It widens the title of the 2023 housing-aid decree so it explicitly covers “how the mortgage is lifted.” It takes effect on publication.

The printed text is an excerpt and does not explain the underlying mortgage or say whether Moroccans resident abroad qualify. Anyone with a stake should check the original 2023 decree.

Source: Decree No. 2.26.578, 22 September 2026, amending Decree No. 2.23.350 of 15 November 2023; issued under Article 8 of Finance Law 50.22 for 2023.

💶 €585.53 million in new foreign financing approved: climate, digital, fish farming

Three decrees approve loans and financing agreements signed with international lenders earlier this year. The combined total is €585.53 million (that sum is ours; the gazette lists each amount separately):

  • €349,100,000 from the World Bank (IBRD, the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development) for the “Climate and Risk Financing” program, signed 30 June 2026.
  • €218,200,000 from the World Bank (loan no. MA9974) for the “Digital Transformation Acceleration in Morocco” program, signed 25 June 2026.
  • €18,230,000 from the Islamic Development Bank, a deferred-payment sale financing deal (an Islamic-finance structure) plus an agency agreement, for a project on aquaculture in inland areas (the Arabic reads “terrestrial areas”), signed 9 August 2026.

These decrees only approve the agreements. The gazette text does not describe what each program will build or how the money will be repaid.

Sources: Decrees No. 2.26.639, 2.26.759 and 2.26.758, all dated 22 September 2026.

🚢 Tanger Med: new container-terminal agreement and amended concession approved

The government has approved two agreements, both signed 31 July 2026, covering a container terminal inside the Tanger Med port on the Mediterranean. The first is between the port’s operating company, Tanger Med Port Authority, and APM Terminals MedPort Tangier. It covers the design, financing, construction, operation and maintenance of a container terminal in the port.

The second is an addendum to the existing concession contract (“TC1”), originally signed on 26 January 2005, between Tanger Med Port Authority and APM Terminals Tangier.

The decree gives no figures on cost, capacity, jobs or duration. The agreements are annexed to the original decree and are not reproduced in this issue, so the real terms are not visible here.

Source: Decree No. 2.26.743, 9 September 2026.

🗺️ Two planning maps approved: Casablanca’s Maarif district and Bir Kandouz

Two urban-development plans, each with binding building rules, were approved and declared to be of public utility. Property owners in these areas should look up the approved plans.

  • Maarif district, Casablanca (plan AUC 01/2026): the public inquiry ran 11 September to 10 October 2025. The Maarif district council and the Casablanca city council each met in late 2025, and a committee reviewed objections and council proposals on 25 March 2026.
  • Bir Kandouz, Aousserd province (plan AUDOE 16/2025, a “major facilities and services zone”): the public inquiry ran 10 October to 9 November 2025. The commune council met on 3 February 2026, and the committee reviewed objections and proposals on 20 May 2026.

Sources: Decrees No. 2.26.658 (Maarif) and No. 2.26.636 (Bir Kandouz), both dated 16 September 2026.

🏅 Anti-doping rules rewritten: targeted testing, blood-spot samples, a conflict-free exemption panel

Morocco has updated its implementing rules for the anti-doping law (Law 97.12). The rules now align testing with the standard of the World Anti-Doping Agency; the gazette prints that agency’s acronym as “DTASS”, which appears garbled. Testing programs must prioritize targeted tests on specific groups of athletes, so limited resources go where risk is highest. Dried blood spot sampling is added: collecting, storing and transporting such samples.

Athletes get a copy of the sampling paperwork they signed, electronically or otherwise. The text also says athletes in the lowest tier of the testing pool do not have to file their whereabouts.

The decree also replaces the rules for the committee that decides therapeutic use exemptions, meaning permission to use a banned substance for medical reasons:

  • The committee must have at least three doctors experienced in treating athletes.
  • Members are appointed by the head of the Moroccan anti-doping agency and cannot be agency officials.
  • Each member must sign a written declaration of no conflict of interest and recuse themselves if one arises.
  • Decisions are by majority, with the chair’s vote deciding ties.

The gazette prints only the amended parts, with dots marking omitted text. The decree was cleared by the Government Council on 18 September 2025, almost exactly a year before it was signed.

Source: Decree No. 2.25.431, 16 September 2026, amending Decree No. 2.18.303 of 5 July 2019.
