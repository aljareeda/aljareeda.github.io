Morocco Extends Free Land Registration in 29 Zones

October 01, 2026
Arabic Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7547, dated 28 September 2026 (16 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

🏡 Six more months of free land registration in 29 zones

If your family owns rural land in Morocco, check this one. The agriculture ministry has extended by six months the window in which owners can complete land-registration steps free of charge in 29 separate “collective registration” zones. These are state-run drives to bring all land in a defined area into the official land register.

The 29 orders were all issued on 24 August 2026. They cover zones in provinces including Taounate, Midelt, Al Haouz, Khenifra, El Jadida, Ouezzane, Safi, M’diq-Fnideq, Larache, Azilal, Sidi Kacem, Khemisset, Driouch, Berrechid, Sefrou, Settat, Sidi Bennour, Errachidia, Moulay Yacoub and Oujda-Angad. Each order names one rural commune. The full list is in the source PDF linked at the bottom.

For diaspora families with inherited or shared land, a registration drive is when ownership gets formally recorded, and the free window is the cheap way to do it. The text only says the free window is extended. It does not give a new end date for each zone or say what changes once the window closes, so confirm the details with the local land registry office. This story also touches inheritance and rural agriculture.

Source: orders of the Minister of Agriculture nos. 1842.26 to 1870.26, dated 24 August 2026 (11 Rabi’ al-Awwal 1448).

⚖️ Governors can now waive or cut the costs of forcing you to pay local debts

The Interior Minister has delegated three powers to regional governors and to provincial and prefectural governors (or the officials they designate). They can: (1) authorise local-government tax collectors to begin forced collection of unpaid debts; (2) appoint the officers who carry out that enforcement; and (3) grant an exemption or reduction of the enforcement costs added on top of debts owed to local authorities.

For ordinary residents, the decision on whether to ease those extra costs now sits with the governor’s office in their own area. The order sets no criteria, thresholds or percentages, so how generously it will be used is unclear. This is a citizen-versus-state accountability story, since the costs tacked onto a debt can be as painful as the debt itself.

Source: Interior Minister order no. 1810.26, 17 August 2026 (3 Rabi’ al-Awwal 1448), under the public-debt collection code (Law 15.97) and the local taxation law (Law 47.06).

🌊 Marchica lagoon (Nador): official development plan approved

The government has approved the official development plan and building rules for the Marchica lagoon site and declared it a matter of public utility. The decree is dated 16 September 2026 and signed by Prime Minister Aziz Akhannouch. It follows a public inquiry from 24 March to 24 April 2026 and special sessions of the councils of Nador, Beni Ansar, Arekmane and Bouarek on 8–14 April 2026. The plan is filed as no. PAS02/AASLM/2026 under the 2010 law governing the lagoon’s development (Law 25.10).

Nador is a region with deep diaspora ties, so this is the document that will shape what can be built around the lagoon. The published text carries no budget, acreage or map, because the plan itself is an attachment.

Source: Decree no. 2.26.744, 16 September 2026 (4 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448).

🎓 Seven foreign degrees accepted for Moroccan equivalence

If you studied abroad, check whether your diploma is on this list. The higher-education minister accepted seven specific foreign qualifications for equivalence with Moroccan degrees:

  • Master’s (MBA): Peking University, China
  • Master’s (applied finance): Bucharest Academy of Economic Studies, Romania
  • Master’s (MBA): Saint Leo University, USA
  • Bachelor’s (economics): Nanchang Institute of Technology, China
  • Bachelor’s (business administration): Istanbul Nisantasi University, Türkiye
  • Bachelor’s (tourism and catering): Eszterhazy Karoly University, Hungary
  • Doctorate (historical sciences, accepted as International Relations): Peoples’ Friendship University of Russia (Patrice Lumumba)

The orders name the qualifying diploma and the Moroccan level it matches. They do not describe the application steps.

Source: Orders of the Minister of Higher Education nos. 1523.26 to 1529.26, 16 July 2026 (1 Safar 1448).

🚄 Land cleared for the Kenitra–Marrakech high-speed line, and a Casablanca motorway interchange

Authorities have approved taking over land to widen the rail corridor for the Kenitra–Marrakech high-speed line, between kilometre markers 466+228 and 474+211. That is just under 8 km, in the commune of Ouled Hassoune Hamri, Rehamna province. The expropriation power is delegated to the director general of the national railway (ONCF). A public inquiry ran from 11 February to 11 April 2026. The project’s public-utility status dates to November 2023 and was renewed in December 2025.

Separately, another order authorises land takeover to rebuild the Sidi Maarouf interchange on the Casablanca ring motorway at km 86+700, in the Ain Chock district.

Source: Transport Minister order no. 1902.26, 24 August 2026; Equipment and Water Minister order no. 1755.26, 6 August 2026.

🏙️ Two regional master plans signed: Dakhla Bay and Berrechid

The government approved the master urban-planning scheme for Oued Eddahab Bay (Dakhla). The councils of Dakhla and El Arkoub held special sessions on 16–17 July 2026. It also approved the scheme for Berrechid province and several communes of Benslimane province. Committee records for that one start in October 2018, so it took nearly eight years to reach a decree. These schemes set the broad rules for where homes, industry and farmland can go. The published text contains no maps or figures.

Source: Decrees nos. 2.26.755 and 2.26.756, 22 September 2026 (10 Rabi’ al-Akhir 1448).

🩺 Cancer-care software merger cleared with five-year conditions

Morocco’s Competition Council allowed Siemens Healthineers to take sole control of Varian Medical Systems, but only with binding commitments. The company must keep its imaging, treatment-planning and oncology-information software compatible with rivals’ products and honour open industry standards (DICOM and DICOM-RT). It must also give non-discriminatory technical support, send the Council half-yearly reports on Morocco, name a local representative and set up a dedicated email address in Morocco for compatibility requests.

The commitments run for five years, renewable once. The Council flagged the risk that Morocco’s still-developing market could become locked into one supplier’s solutions.

Source: Competition Council decision no. 123/26, 22 June 2026 (6 Muharram 1448).

💨 Deals cleared: a 104 MW Laayoune wind farm, MSC, Turkish Airlines and a tomato grower

  • Wind: Eco Wind SA, part of Ynna Holding, buys 50% of Tarouma Wind 1 SA, which develops a 104-megawatt wind plant in Laayoune. It joins the existing shareholder, AMEA Energy Investments 20 DMCC, a UAE-registered company.
  • Shipping: MSC group’s SAS Shipping Agencies Services takes exclusive control of Centre Imex SAS and its subsidiaries.
  • Air travel: Turkish Airlines gets joint control of Air Europa Holding alongside Globalia. The Council defined the passenger markets as the Marrakech–Istanbul and Marrakech–Athens routes, and noted the target’s share was 1% or less.
  • Agriculture: Quality Tomato Nederland BV takes control of Omagreen SARL, an Agadir company farming about 80 hectares of leased land planted with carob trees.
  • Ground handling: Swissport Maroc takes control of Swiftair Maroc.

Source: Competition Council decisions nos. 120/26, 121/26, 128/26, 135/26, 136/26, 19 June to 7 July 2026.
