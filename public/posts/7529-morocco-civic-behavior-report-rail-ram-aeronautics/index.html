Morocco's Own Watchdog Says Street Violence Is Up 29% — And Nobody's Punished for It

July 27, 2026
Arabic Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7529, dated 27 July 2026 (12 Safar 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

📊 Morocco’s Economic, Social and Environmental Council: Public Spaces Are Getting Less Civil, and the Data Backs It Up

The lead story this issue isn’t a new law — it’s a candid, statistics-heavy opinion from the Economic, Social and Environmental Council (CESE), Morocco’s official advisory body on social and economic policy, published in full in the gazette. It amounts to a state institution publicly admitting that everyday public order is deteriorating, and it comes packed with hard numbers rarely aired this openly.

Drawing on police and gendarmerie data from 2021–2024, the report shows violence-related crimes in urban areas jumped from 46,553 cases in 2023 to 60,054 in 2024 — a roughly 29% one-year increase — with harassment complaints up even faster. Attacks on private property rose from 25,368 to 35,168 cases (about 38%) over the same period. In rural and semi-rural areas, gendarmerie figures show 52,670 cases logged over the reporting period, 97% of them crimes against people rather than property, involving 46,333 people — mostly men, largely unemployed or in precarious day-labor jobs. Road safety indicators are similarly troubling: gendarmerie-recorded traffic offenses hit nearly 700,000 over the period, national traffic accidents rose about 15% between 2023 and 2024, and pedestrians made up close to a quarter of all 2024 road deaths, with motorcycle, moped and three-wheeler riders accounting for another 42%. Littering alone made up 86% of the 60,333 environmental violations recorded over four years, and prisons remain disproportionately filled with young people (about 76% of inmates).

The Council also commissioned its own nationwide survey of 1,012 people, finding that only 7% rate current civic behavior as “very high,” while a striking 86% support tougher penalties for uncivil conduct in public spaces and just 9% oppose them. The report’s recommendations lean on a mix of public education, stiffer enforcement, and infrastructure fixes targeted at the spaces people flagged as worst: public facilities, streets and public transport. Because the report touches both public accountability (how the state polices and protects citizens) and hard statistics, it ranks as the top story this issue on both counts. Source: CESE opinion, adopted 22 December 2025, published in Bulletin Officiel No. 7529 (27 July 2026).

🚄 More Land Seized for the Kenitra–Marrakech High-Speed Rail Extension

Morocco’s Transport Ministry has amended an earlier order authorizing the state to take ownership of additional land plots needed to extend the high-speed rail line (“TGV”) from Kenitra to Marrakech — this batch covering parcels in the Harbil commune, Marrakech province, in the Marrakech-Safi region, between specific kilometer markers along the planned route. A separate, related order does the same for parcels near Sala Al Jadida, in the Rabat-Salé-Kénitra region. These are technical, incremental land-acquisition steps rather than a new project announcement, but they confirm the southward extension of Morocco’s flagship high-speed line beyond its current Tangier–Kenitra–Casablanca route is actively moving forward. Source: Decisions No. 929.26 (6 May 2026) and No. 981.26 (14 May 2026), Ministry of Transport and Logistics.

✈️ Royal Air Maroc Launches an Aircraft-Maintenance Training Academy

The government has authorized RAM (Royal Air Maroc) to create a new subsidiary, “Moroccan Academy of Aeronautics SA,” with an initial capital of 300,000 dirhams. The academy’s job is to manage training for aircraft maintenance technicians, airline operations staff and technical aviation support — and its explicit target is to more than triple the number of maintenance and repair technicians Morocco trains each year, from current levels to over 550 annually. The move is tied to two agreements RAM signed in December 2025 and is framed as support for the airline’s “Program Contract” running through 2037, which envisions a much larger RAM fleet and more in-house maintenance capacity rather than relying on outside providers. Source: Decree No. 2.26.535, dated 23 July 2026 (8 Safar 1448).

🌾 Import Duty Breaks on Wheat and Meat Extended — With a Catch

Two decrees extend relief on food import duties, aimed at keeping a lid on prices for staples. The suspension of import duty on soft wheat and its derivatives is extended through 31 August 2026, after which the duty itself is dropped entirely from 1 September 2026 onward, rather than merely paused. Separately, the suspension of import duty on live sheep and on the meat of cattle, sheep, goats and camels is extended through 31 December 2026 — though the duty on offal from these same animals is not waived, just capped at a 30% rate through the same date. Both measures update decrees first issued earlier this year. Source: Decrees No. 2.26.583 and No. 2.26.584, both dated 23 July 2026 (8 Safar 1448).

🌊 Tighzirt Dam: More Farmland Handed Over in Béni Mellal

The Ministry of Equipment and Water has authorized the state to take ownership of additional land parcels in the Naour commune, Béni Mellal province, needed to continue building the Tighzirt Dam on the Oued Derna river. This is a follow-up to a public-utility declaration first issued in 2022 and renewed in 2024, and the affected plots and owners are listed in an attached table rather than summarized in the text. The dam is part of Morocco’s ongoing push to expand water storage capacity in the central plains amid recurring drought pressure, though the gazette entry itself doesn’t give a reservoir capacity or completion date. Source: Decision No. 1279.26, dated 18 June 2026 (2 Muharram 1448).
