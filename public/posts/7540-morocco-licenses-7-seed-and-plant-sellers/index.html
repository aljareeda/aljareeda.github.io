Morocco Licenses 7 Seed and Plant Sellers, Then Makes Them Report Sales

September 03, 2026
French Edition

Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco’s Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7540, dated 3 September 2026 (21 rabii I 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.

🌱 Seven Companies Can Now Sell Certified Seeds and Plants — and Must Report Every Sale

If your family farms olives, argan, figs or saffron in Morocco, the nursery selling you young trees must now be officially approved, and it must tell the state what it sells. The Agriculture Minister approved seven companies to market certified seeds and plants. Each licence lasts five years from the day it is published in the gazette. It can be renewed for another five years, but the company must ask at least six months before expiry.

The approval comes with a paper trail. Each company has to report its purchases, sales and, for most crops, its stock to the National Food Safety Office (ONSSA), the state body that oversees food safety. The schedule depends on the crop:

  • Olive plants: purchases and sales reported every April and September.
  • Vines, apple/pear-type trees and fig trees: the same April and September schedule, with stocks included.
  • Pomegranate and red-fruit plants (strawberry, raspberry, blueberry and similar): stock reports every November and May.
  • Citrus: reports every January and July.
  • Saffron bulbs: reports on production, sales and stocks before the end of May each year.
  • Prickly pear, stone-fruit, carob and argan plants: stock reports at least once a year, by 31 December. For prickly pear the source says “annually” without giving a date.
  • Field seeds (maize, legumes, oilseeds, vegetables): sales reports every month. Potato plants are reported every six months, and straw cereals such as wheat and barley once a year, at the end of December.

Any company that is found to have broken the seed and plant law can lose its approval. The gazette does not name specific penalties or fines.

The seven approved companies and their listed head offices:

Overlap: agriculture, food safety and business climate. Sources: Ministerial orders no. 1317-26 to 1321-26, 1323-26 and 1324-26, dated 23 June 2026.

⚓ Marchica 1 Marina Gets Its Official Operating Rules

The Minister of Equipment and Water, Nizar Baraka, has approved the operating rules for the Marchica 1 marina, part of the Marchica lagoon development area. The rules cover how the marina is run day to day. The gazette prints only the short approval order. The rules themselves are an annex that is not reproduced in this issue, so we cannot say what they contain, such as berth fees or boating restrictions. The Arabic text was published in issue no. 7538 of 27 August 2026.

Source: Ministerial order no. 1008-26, dated 21 May 2026.
